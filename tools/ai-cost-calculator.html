Why input and output pricing must be separated

Many hosted language models price input and output tokens differently. Mixing them into one “per token” number hides an important lever: a workload that generates long answers can be materially more expensive than a workload that mostly sends context.

Use the calculator for scenarios, not false precision

Model the workload under low, expected, and high assumptions. Real bills can also include cached-input discounts, batch pricing, tool calls, image/audio tokens, provider-specific minimums, or other metered units. Those should be added as separate line items once you know the provider rules.
